# Flows, onboarding, progress, endings

## Winning rules
- **One purpose per screen (UX law 1).** Break a hard decision into small steps. Recommend an option when users may struggle to choose.
- **Show progress (laws 11, 20; C3).** In real multi-step flows, show completed and unfinished steps ("Step 2 of 4", a bar, or a checklist). Show progress already made. Make the remaining work specific. Decorative section numbering (01 / 02 / 03) stays banned.
- **Save progress and make resume easy.** (UX law 11)
- **Put essentials first and last (UX law 9).** Most important item first. Final action or takeaway last. Lower-priority items in the middle.
- **Shortest path (UX law 18).** Fewest steps. Prefill. Remove needless screens. Offer shortcuts for frequent actions.
- **Onboarding gets users to first value fast.** Its job is not to teach the product. It is to reach the moment that proves the product is worth the time. Design empty states as part of onboarding. (S3 onboard)
- **End memorably (UX law 10).** A clear completion state: what was done, what happens next. Never end on an empty or unclear screen.
- **Do not leave users to remember** information between screens. (UX law 5)
- **Critical user journeys.** Write the key journeys with `/ux-spec` (stored under `.ux/cujs/`). Replay them against the running app with `/cuj-audit`. The audit names the exact step that broke. (S1)
