# Copy and microcopy

## Winning rules
- **Write the argument first (Persuade).** List the page as claims with proof, one line per section. Delete any section with no claim. This avoids the stock order of hero, logos, features, testimonial, pricing, call to action. (taste copy.md)
- **Say what this is.** The first screen has one `h1` that says what it is, who it is for, and what to do next. Every section has a heading. (taste, UX law 1)
- **Controls name their action.** "Save changes", not "Submit". Do not use "Get started" or "Learn more" as the only label when a specific verb exists. (taste, craft-floor)
- **Errors name the problem and the recovery.** (craft-floor, UX law 15)
- **Use the user's language,** not internal jargon. Keep terms the same across screens. (S3 clarify)
- **Plain section names.** No performative labels ("Field notes", "Quietly trusted by"). No eyebrows above headings, no pill badge above the headline. (taste tells, craft-floor)
- **Generated-sounding words:** avoid unlock, supercharge, seamless, elevate, effortless, cutting-edge, all-in-one; the "not just X, it's Y" correction; tricolon headlines; lorem ipsum. (taste copy tells)
- **No invented proof.** No made-up logos, quotes, star rows, or "10,000+ teams". Use real proof or none. Claims and configuration come from supplied truth. Label illustrative values. (taste, craft-floor)
- **Ask before changing factual or legal copy.** (S3 clarify)
- **End well.** Confirm what the user did and what happens next. (UX law 10)
- **Headline length (C8):** keep it to two lines unless clarity needs more. Then add a short sentence under it.
