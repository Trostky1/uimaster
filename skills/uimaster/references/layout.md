# Layout, spacing, cards, radius

## Winning rules
- **Group by space.** Related items close, unrelated items far. More space above a heading than below it. The gap between sections is clearly larger than any gap inside one. (UX laws 4, 17; craft-floor)
- **Connect deliberately.** Use containers, lines, or shared backgrounds to show relation. Do not use them as decoration. (UX law 17)
- **Similar look, similar job.** Same component, same look and behavior. Do not give different actions the same treatment. (UX law 16)
- **Frame:** equal outer margins. No section huddled on one side with the other half empty. No blank stretch taller than half a screen (one deliberate rest per page at most). (taste house rules)
- **Reading order:** squint test. With detail blurred, you can still find the primary element, then the secondary, then the groups. (S3 layout)
- **Cards (C5):** a card is for a distinct record: a plan, a product, a person. Never nest cards. Never use same-size icon-plus-heading-plus-text cards as the page structure. (craft-floor, taste, UX law 17)
- **Radius (C4):** choose the radius once for the system, from the brand world. No fixed 12-16px rule, and no "one medium radius on everything" by habit. Declare elevation once: border or shadow, not both. Pills are for small controls. (taste, craft-floor)
- **Lines (C11):** Persuade pages use space, tone steps, and filled shapes instead of hairlines. App screens may use quiet borders. Tables and form fields may always have lines. (taste, app-screens)
- **Operate and Read:** predictable structure and stable density are affordances. (S3 layout)
- **Persuade and Experience:** vary the arrangement section by section. The same layout family in every section is a tell. (taste)
- **Hierarchy at a glance:** one dominant element per section. Remove visual noise. (UX law 12 Prägnanz)
